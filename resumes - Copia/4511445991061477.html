Interoperable Research Infrastructure for Managing Sustainable Grid Cells
Descrição: The project Interoperable Research Infrastructure for Managing Sustainable Grid Cells (IRI4SGC) is a joint research project from partners from the European Union (EU) and the Latin American and Caribbean (LAC) region. The six participating universities Bielefeld University of Applied Sciences, Graz University of Technology, Federal University of Amazonas Brazil, Universidad Mayor de San Andrés Bolivia, Universidad Tecnológica de Panamá and the associated partner university Universidad Tecnológica La Salle Nicaragua investigate the inter- and transdisciplinary challenges of technical and economic integration of decentralized energy systems.Clean energy transition with a high share of renewable energy can lead to problems in the grid security of the electrical grids. This is due to volatile feed-in of renewable energy with simultaneous high loads from dynamic consumers, e.g. electric vehicles, as well as prosumers. To avoid this, autonomous control of energy cells and recommendations for actions and algorithms for the grid operators are being developed. A highly versatile and scalable infrastructure is needed to reproduce and test an exact image of the real grid. For this purpose, the existing laboratories will be virtually connected in this project and a secure energy data exchange environment will be established. All the participating parties are able to operate the joint RI remotely and profit from the knowledge and experience of the other partners. This new collaborative approach supports the idea of durable and sustainable products and helps reduce the need for resources. The cross-continental connection between the EU and LAC regions and the exchange of competences and expertise are at the focus of addressing the challenges of the clean energy transition in all participating andconsidered regions.Chamada Edital: RESOLUÇÃO N. 013/2022 - EU-LAC 2022.

Smart biosolar microgrids for sustainable communities
Descrição: The project investigates the integration of anaerobic digestion (AD) with solar photovoltaic (PV) to form the basis of a circular economy model, providing affordable, clean, and fair energy access. We will adopt an interdisciplinary approach combining engineering and social science to develop solutions that are appropriate to the local characteristics of the beneficiary communities. Hybrid microgrids (MG), combining different forms of energy supply, have been studied and demonstrated as a solution to increase the resilience and effectiveness of the provision of energy. The concept is usually restricted to PV with diesel backup, while there is a scarcity of knowledge for PV and bioenergy integration. Especially for rural locations with an abundance of bioresources, bioenergy can play an important role in stabilising the power supply when solar production decreases or in periods of high demand. The need to constantly balance demand and supply of electricity, together with the combination of different and spatially separated components is a technical challenge of hybrid MGs. The project will therefore investigate a novel control approach based on a cooperative distributed structure, with local controllers for each component of the hybrid MG system; compared to centralised approaches, this potentially delivers higher reliability and facilitates the addition of components in future expansions. Together with the technical dimension, we consider the social aspects that transform the energy supply into energy services, and finally into social values. We will therefore co-design a methodology, which integrates technical expert knowledge, with stakeholders? perceptions and aspirations, and local economic and governmental aspects. This would allow the design of sustainable business models, able to capture multiple values and needs, such as access to energy, improved health from waste treatment, use of digestate as fertiliser, community cohesion, and novel enterprises..

Robust solar powered storage systems with second-life batteries and their economic and ecological potential
Descrição: The aim of the project is to establish a research network in the area of solar-powered storage systems basedon second-life batteries from automotive applications. These storage solutions can be used especially in theremote areas that do not yet have electricity connection and are therefore particularly suitable for settlements in the Amazon region. For the development of such systems, a number of multidisciplinary research questions, such as the remaining service life, the system reliability or the economic and ecological potential, need to be solved. An already existing cooperation between Prof. Hans-Georg Schweiger (Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt, THI) and Prof. Joaquim M. Gonçalves (Universidade Federal de Santa Catarina, UFSC) in the field of thermal behaviour of high-voltage batteries will be used as a basis for the development of the future research network. This cooperation will now be extended to other researchers with expertise in the relevant areas at THI, UFSC, the Instituto Federal de Santa Catarina (IFSC), the Universidade Federal do Amazonas (UFAM) and the University of Duisburg-Essen (UDE).As an outcome of the DAAD funding, an international multidisciplinary research network with a high level oftechnical and economical expertise in the field of solar-powered storage systems based on second-life batteries for stationary applications will be established comprising three Brazilian (IFSC, UFAM, UFSC) and two German universities (THI, UDE). In addition to the university partners, partners from industry and public authorities and organizations, such as the Brazilian Electricity Regulatory Agency (ANEEL) as well as the German Society for International Cooperation (GIZ) will also be involved. As a result of the cooperation project, ideas for subsequent application-oriented research projects between Brazilian and German universities and industry will to be elaborated and applied for to funding authorities in response to available research grant opportunities. Within the framework of the research network, a transfer of newly developed research results on the electrical and mechanical behavior of second-life batteries from automotive use, their thermal behavior and corresponding thermal simulation methods, the climatic conditions in Amazon region and lifetime estimation methods will be transferred into concrete project ideas for the development of applications and design proposals together with industry partners. Workshops in Brazil and Germany with university partners, industry representatives and authorities will be carried out for the exchange of research results and elaboration of future research activities. Research exchanges at Brazilian and German partner universities will be carried out to promote technology transfer and to do preparations for future joint research activities. In addition, there will be an opportunity for several master's students to complete part of their master's thesis abroad. The DAAD NoPa 2.0 funding will be also used to motivate other students to use regular DAAD and CAPES funding programs for exchange to spend part of their PhD or masters program at a partner university abroad. Within the framework of the cooperation, the collaboration between the universities and their international offices will be further strengthened at the administrative level. The already existing cooperation at the strategic network AWARE (Applied Network on Automotive Research and Education) between THI and UFSC will be used as basis for future cooperation with IFSC, UFAM and UDE.The proposal is to be classified into the area of Direct Electrification and Energy Storage Isolated System Battery Technologies..

Electrifying the Amazon: the impacts of access to renewable energy in riverine communities
Descrição: This project aims to evaluate the socio-economic impacts of two alternative configurations of solar photovoltaic systems in riverine communities and provide recommendations for expanding access to other communities in the Brazilian state of Amazonas. Such communities typically rely on dieselgenerators for the provision of electricity, which can hinder development and generate negative environmental impacts. This collaboration builds upon two previous Newton Fund Institutional Links grants. The project focuses on communities enrolled in Programa Bolsa Floresta (PBF), a policy that aims to protect tropical forests and reduce poverty. Enacted by the State of Amazonas in 2007 and managed by the Amazonas Sustainable Foundation (FAS), PBF has to date enrolled almost 600 poor,riverine communities that manage 16 million hectares of forest. To help meet the aims of PBF, the policy develops alternative livelihoods and sources of income that reduce pressure on forests, such as handicrafts, often dominated by women. Expanding access to and improving the quality of supply of electricity can help increase the scope and scale of these activities thus potentially improving incomes, enhancing the role of women in local economies, strengthening participation in environmental governance and encouraging more inclusive economic growth in Amazon communities. We will evaluate energy demand, incomes, gender implications and greenhouse gas emissions for two different configurations of solar photovoltaic systems: centralized and decentralized. The latter was installed in a previous Newton Fund project undertaken in riverine communities ('Sustainable and Replicable Off-grid Renewable Energy System for Riverside Communities in the Amazon, Brazil-STAR Energy'). The former is to be trialled in this project. Both will be evaluated using methodological tools developed in a second Newton Fund project ('Participatory Approaches to Natural Resource Conservation in the Brazilian Amazon').(projeto cancelado por causa da Pandemia de COVID-19 e da impossibilidade de acesso às comunidades ribeirinhas nos anos de 2020 e 2021).

Influência de grandezas físicas e químicas nas tecnologias de painéis solares fotovoltaicos e seu impacto na eficiência da geração de energia de sistemas distribuídos
Descrição: A energia solar fotovoltaica é a que mais cresce no mundo. A Agência Internacional de Energia destacou em seu relatório um aumento de mais de 80 nas adições globais de capacidade solar fotovoltaica. As mudanças nas matrizes elétricas apontam a direção das fontes de energia renováveis, com a energia solar liderando. Os painéis podem ser instalados sobre os tetos de prédios e residências, estacionamentos, campos naturais e ou sobre os imensos lagos. Pode-se aproveitar estruturas já prontas e sem a demanda de novas áreas ou de desflorestamento, o que representa uma alternativa interessante. Entretanto, hoje células solares e, mais especificamente, os painéis solares apresentam uma eficiência de conversão fotovoltaica abaixo de 25. Várias rotas tecnológicas e soluções surgiram nestes anos, algumas funcionando apenas em laboratório e outras já no mercado. Como as opções são muitas, este projeto visa investigar e indicar a solução, ou as soluções, que apresentem o melhor resultado considerando questões técnicas, ambientais e econômicas. Ao final do projeto, um relatório executivo será gerado indicando o que uma empresa do setor deve apostar para ter um produto de melhor eficiência de conversão da energia. Faz parte do Programa Institucional de Bolsas de Iniciação em Desenvolvimento Tecnológico e Inovação (PIBITI) da UFAM.

2024 - 2025
Influência de grandezas físicas e químicas nas tecnologias de painéis solares fotovoltaicos e seu impacto na eficiência da geração de energia de sistemas distribuídos
Descrição: A energia solar fotovoltaica é a que mais cresce no mundo. A Agência Internacional de Energia destacou em seu relatório um aumento de mais de 80 nas adições globais de capacidade solar fotovoltaica. As mudanças nas matrizes elétricas apontam a direção das fontes de energia renováveis, com a energia solar liderando. Os painéis podem ser instalados sobre os tetos de prédios e residências, estacionamentos, campos naturais e ou sobre os imensos lagos. Pode-se aproveitar estruturas já prontas e sem a demanda de novas áreas ou de desflorestamento, o que representa uma alternativa interessante. Entretanto, hoje células solares e, mais especificamente, os painéis solares apresentam uma eficiência de conversão fotovoltaica abaixo de 25. Várias rotas tecnológicas e soluções surgiram nestes anos, algumas funcionando apenas em laboratório e outras já no mercado. Como as opções são muitas, este projeto visa investigar e indicar a solução, ou as soluções, que apresentem o melhor resultado considerando questões técnicas, ambientais e econômicas. Ao final do projeto, um relatório executivo será gerado indicando o que uma empresa do setor deve apostar para ter um produto de melhor eficiência de conversão da energia. Faz parte do Programa Institucional de Bolsas de Iniciação em Desenvolvimento Tecnológico e Inovação (PIBITI) da UFAM.
Situação: Concluído; Natureza: Desenvolvimento.
